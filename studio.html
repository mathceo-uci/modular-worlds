The Studio

On the ship's lower deck, Lucía turned an empty cargo hold into a painting studio. Every canvas here is painted by a rule from the voyage. You choose the colours and turn the dials; the arithmetic does the brushwork.

There are no right answers in the Studio. Play until something surprises you, then ask the crew's favourite question: why does it look like that?

How to keep a painting

Press Make a still under any canvas. The picture appears below the controls: right-click it (or press and hold on a phone) to save it. Share your best ones with your crew, along with the settings that made them.

Pascal's carpet

Pascal's triangle, each number replaced by its leftover mod m. With m = 2 a famous fractal appears: Sierpiński's triangle. Compare a prime modulus (3, 5, 7) with a non-prime one (4, 6, 9). What changes?

Times-table circle

Put n points around a clock. Join each point k to point a·k mod n. Times 2 draws a heart-shaped cardioid; times 3 a three-petal curve. Try 21, 34, 51 and 99 with 200 points.

Colours: first line, last line, background
Chapter 3, Euclid's Forge

Euclid's quilt

Cut the biggest squares you can from an a × b cloth, then repeat on what is left. That is Euclid's algorithm, sewn: each colour is one step, the number of squares in a colour is a quotient, and the smallest square is the gcd. Try neighbouring Fibonacci numbers (89 × 55).

Number spiral

Write 1, 2, 3, … in a square spiral, and colour each number by its leftover mod m. Stripes and diagonals appear. Then light up a Book from the voyage, or the primes, and look for the lines they like.

More rooms

The Studio has three more rooms, each with its own art form:

The Sound Room: open now

The 12 notes of a piano octave are the integers mod 12, and Euclid's algorithm keeps the beat for the tresillo, the cumbia and the bossa nova. Enter the Sound Room

The Poets' Corner: open now

The sestina moves six end-words by one fixed shuffle, and Fibonacci poems rise and fall like tides mod m. Write within the rule. Enter the Poets' Corner

The Loom: open now

Will a bracelet's pattern close up, and after how many laps? Which satin weaves hold together? Beads, cloth, lcm and gcd. Enter the Loom
