The Proof Lab

Behind the harbor in Mojadito, Lucía found an old laboratory. Every flask holds a number, and the shelves are labelled with recipes: produce an even from two odds, produce a full trio from one extra and one short, and later in the voyage, pack crates of d into crates of d. The rules of the lab are the rules of arithmetic. Follow them, and you produce a proof.

Each recipe can be produced in more than one way. Find a route, pass Kevin's safety inspection, and the lab writes up your report.

The lab report

Distillate: the essence of your proof in plain words, no symbols.

Procedure: the formal proof, one line per move, each with its reason.

Result: the theorem you have produced, for every number of its kind, not just the examples.

Recipes

Pick a recipe. Proving one adds it to your lab rules, so later recipes can use it. Your progress stays in this browser only.

How the lab works

Flasks are numbers. A card like a is a number you know one fact about (odd, even, one extra). Reading its label means using the definition: an odd number is some number of pairs plus one loose atom. The dots stand for any number of pairs, including none.

Moves are reasons. Every move is a rule of arithmetic or a result you proved earlier. The lab never asks you to follow a fixed path. It only checks that each move is legal, and that your final flask shows what the recipe asks for.

Kevin inspects. He looks for the classic mistakes: using the same letter for two different numbers, using the claim to prove itself, and forgetting the case with no pairs at all.

From report to proof

The procedure is a scaffold. A finished proof in a paper or on an exam is a short paragraph. After each report, rewrite it in your own words, in English, Spanish or both. Start from your Distillate: it tells you what the paragraph is about.

Crews can swap report images and inspect each other's reasons, the way Kevin does.
