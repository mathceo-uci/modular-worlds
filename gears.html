The Gear Shop

In the smiths' quarter of Euclid's Forge, an old clockmaker sells toothed rings and wheels. Put a pen through a hole in a small wheel, roll it around inside a big ring, and a flower appears. How many petals? When does the pen come home? The answers are a gcd and an lcm.

The gear rule

A wheel with r teeth rolling in a ring with R teeth draws a figure with R ÷ gcd(R, r) petals, and closes after r ÷ gcd(R, r) trips around the ring.

Why it closes: count teeth

Each time the wheel rolls one tooth, one tooth of the ring has been used. The pen is back where it started exactly when the wheel has turned a whole number of times and the contact point is back at the start of the ring. So the number of teeth rolled must be a multiple of r and of R: the first time is lcm(R, r) teeth.

That many teeth is lcm ÷ R trips around the ring, and lcm ÷ r turns of the wheel. Each turn of the wheel draws one petal. Since lcm(R, r) = Rr ÷ gcd(R, r), you get the gear rule.

Challenges

  1. Find a ring and wheel that make exactly 5 petals. Then find three more pairs that also make 5.
  2. Which wheel gives the most petals for a ring of 96? Which gives the fewest (other than a full-size wheel)?
  3. With R = 105 and r = 64, how many trips before the pen comes home? Predict, then press Draw slowly.
  4. Navigator: prove that the petals for (R, r) and for (R, R − r) are the same number.
