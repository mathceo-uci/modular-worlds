A playful first course in number theory

Modular Worlds

Six students, one ship, and an archipelago where every island's chemistry hides a theorem. Eleven lands, ten weeks: parity and divisibility, Euclid, Diophantine equations, primes, congruences, the Chinese Remainder Theorem, Fermat, RSA and sums of two squares.

Written for math and computer science majors in a lower-division course. No proofs course is needed: the proof toolkit is built in, one chapter at a time.

The voyage, chapter by chapter

Each chapter is one land. Most take one week of the course; Chapters 1 and 2 share Week 2. Chapters 0 to 3 are ready to read.

How this book works

Prophecies

Chapter 1 seals a Vault of conjectures about the whole voyage. Each chapter opens some of them. Breaking a prophecy, and repairing it, counts as a discovery.

Three paths

Every chapter's problems come in three tiers. Deckhand problems are for everyone; Navigator problems stretch your proofs; Cartographer problems are open explorations.

Arenas and episodes

Each land has a game you can play on the page and in class, and opens with a scene from the crew's voyage. The games always pit the whole room against El Gran Pesado, never students against each other.

Code quests

Optional Python cells that run right in the page. Turn a theorem into code, and turn experiments into new conjectures. No installation needed.

The Proof Lab

Produce proofs like a chemist: read the labels, bond loose atoms, use rules you proved before. Every route you find comes with a lab report: the essence in plain words, then the formal proof. Open the Proof Lab.

The Studio and its rooms

Twelve rooms where the voyage's arithmetic becomes art, music, poetry, weaving, juggling, gears, calendars, ciphers, pictures and games. The first rooms need nothing beyond Chapter 0: poems for two voices, stanza crates, and the multiples machine. See all the rooms.

For instructors

The course runs as three 50-minute sessions a week: Expedition (explore and story), Forge (theory and proof) and Arena (games, proof wrestling and the land's Boss Problem).

Instructor guide, Week 3: Euclid's Forge: timings, how to run the balance and the game in class, common mistakes, and answer keys.
